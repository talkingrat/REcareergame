£1 million, ten years, and a property market where different sectors move in different directions. Read the yields, spot the sectors on the rise, and build the biggest fortune.

The whole game in one line
A property's price is its yearly rent ÷ its yield. When a sector's yield falls, its prices rise — and different sectors rise and fall at different times. Buy the sectors on the way up, sell before they turn, and don't get caught over-borrowed.

How Yield Works

Price = Rent ÷ Yield
a shop earning £70,000 rent at a 7% yield is worth £70,000 ÷ 0.07 = £1,000,000
"Yield" is the yearly rent as a % of price — your return. High yield = cheap (lots of rent per pound). Low yield = expensive.
Prices move when yields move
Same £70,000 rent, different market moods:
Optimistic — yield 5%£1,400,000
Normal — yield 7%£1,000,000
Nervous — yield 8%£875,000
The rent never changed — the price swung £525,000 on yield alone.
🔀
Sectors move differently
Offices and high-street shops might be sliding while retail parks, warehouses and flats are climbing — all at once. Watch each sector's yield and back the winners. Warehouses (industrial) swing the hardest, up and down.
🏦
You can borrow (leverage)
Put down some cash, borrow the rest (up to 70%). Rent helps pay the interest. Borrowing multiplies gains when prices rise — and losses when they fall.
🏗️
You can build (development)
Start a development: it costs cash for ~3 years with no rent while it's built, and might overrun. If it finishes, it's worth far more than it cost — the biggest prize, and the biggest risk.
